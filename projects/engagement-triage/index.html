What's an engagement tracker?

An engagement is any request for a leader's time: a speaking invitation, a customer meeting, an interview, a panel, a school visit, a video message. A tracker is the shared record of every request, where it stands, and what was decided.

Most offices start with an inbox and a spreadsheet. That works until the volume grows. Then requests get buried, two people answer the same email, and nobody can say what's still waiting for a decision.

The problem I was solving

I supported a senior executive at Microsoft whose office received a steady stream of requests by email. Keeping track of them took three separate manual processes, so the same details were typed in more than once and every update took time.

The slow part wasn't storing requests. It was reading them. Someone had to open every email thread and find the event, the date, who was asking and what they wanted before anyone could decide.

The one rule

These requests go to a senior leader, so a wrong date or a made-up detail costs more than the time any automation saves. The rule was set before anything was built: the human is always the final sender. AI can read, sort and draft. A person decides and sends.

How it works, one request at a time

  1. An invitation arrives in the team's shared inbox. Say it's a regional business council asking your leader to speak at their annual dinner.
  2. The tracker reads it. AI pulls out the event name, date, who's asking, their organization and what they want, and writes a two-sentence summary. It all lands as one row in a shared list.
  3. Monday morning, the team gets one email listing every open request with its summary. No one has to open every thread to know what's waiting.
  4. Someone makes the call and changes the request's status to Accepted or Decline.
  5. If it's a no, a polite decline is drafted and saved to the Drafts folder. A person reads it, edits it if needed, and sends it.
  6. If it's a yes, the details are poured into your briefing template, so the prep document starts mostly filled in.

The same idea, used for meeting follow-ups

After a large industry conference, the executive had a run of customer meetings that each needed a follow-up email. I applied the same pattern: notes went into a simple form, AI drafted the follow-up email in the executive's usual format, and a person reviewed and sent each one. Each recap took about 10 minutes, roughly 70% less time, and all 11 follow-ups went out on time in a consistent format.

No Microsoft 365? The idea carries over. Any shared spreadsheet or database, an automation tool like Zapier or Make, and an AI step will do the same jobs. Start with the list and the weekly email. Those two alone fix most of the problem.
