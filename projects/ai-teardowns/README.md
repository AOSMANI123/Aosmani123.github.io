# AI Product Teardowns

## PRFAQ

### Press release

**AI Product Teardowns: A structured framework for analyzing shipped AI products through a product management lens.**

Most AI product commentary falls into two buckets: technical deep-dives on model architecture, or surface-level takes on user experience. Neither helps a PM think about what to build next.

AI Product Teardowns fills the gap. Each teardown applies a consistent five-part framework — metrics tree, funnel analysis, feature kill, feature build, and falsifiability conditions — to a shipped AI product. The result is analysis specific enough to be wrong, which is the only kind worth reading.

The teardowns cover ChatGPT, GitHub Copilot, Notion AI, Midjourney, and Grammarly. Each one takes a specific position: ChatGPT has a retention problem masked by growth numbers. Copilot's headline metric (acceptance rate) doesn't measure what matters. Notion AI cannibalizes its own core loop. Midjourney's distribution advantage is becoming a constraint. Grammarly's AI rewrite features undermine the trust model that made the product work.

### FAQ

**Q: Why teardowns instead of case studies?**
A case study describes what happened. A teardown takes a position on what should happen next. The goal is to practice the kind of thinking a PM does every day: look at a product, form a hypothesis about what's working and what isn't, and name the bet you'd make.

**Q: Why these five products?**
They represent different stages and strategies in AI product development. ChatGPT is a general-purpose tool trying to find specific use cases. Copilot is AI embedded in an existing workflow. Notion AI is AI bolted onto a platform. Midjourney is an AI-native product with an unconventional distribution channel. Grammarly is a pre-AI product adding AI capabilities. Each one surfaces different product problems.

**Q: How are the metrics trees constructed?**
By working backward from what the company likely optimizes for (revenue, engagement, retention) and decomposing into the driver and input metrics that a product team can actually influence. The trees are hypothesized, not sourced from internal data.

**Q: How should someone evaluate whether these teardowns are good?**
Three tests. First, are the positions specific enough to be falsifiable? "ChatGPT should improve retention" is too vague. "ChatGPT's empty prompt box causes 40%+ of new users to bounce before their first query" is testable. Second, does the metrics tree make the product's strategy legible? Third, would the feature recommendations actually move the identified metric?

**Q: What's the intended audience?**
Anyone who evaluates product thinking: PM hiring managers, other PMs, and anyone learning to think about AI products as products rather than as technology demonstrations.
