Coverage gap detector for executive news briefs

Building a measurable quality metric for a fuzzy editorial problem: did today's brief actually cover what was available, or did it just look like it did?

Built October 2026Type Evaluation toolStack Python · RSS · News APIs

Short version. A daily executive news brief is only as good as the stories it catches. This tool measures coverage by sweeping public sources for a roster of entities over a time window, then scoring any candidate brief against what was actually available. The output is a coverage score, an entity-by-entity breakdown, and a list of stories the brief missed that multiple sources carried.

The problem

A daily news brief for a senior leader looks fine if you only read it forwards. The articles are sourced, the summaries are accurate, the format is clean. But "is this brief correct?" and "is this brief complete?" are different questions, and most quality checks only answer the first one.

Completeness is harder to measure because you need to know what was available before you can judge what was missed. And the misses come in two flavors:

Class of missWhat it meansExample
Roster definition problemYou're tracking the wrong entities entirelyThe brief tracks 50 companies but the 3 most relevant ones aren't on the list
Search-breadth problemYou're tracking the right entities but not finding stories that existedA CEO resigned, 4 major outlets covered it, but the brief didn't include it because the search didn't reach those sources

You can't fix what you can't measure. This tool measures both.

What it does

  1. Input a roster of entities to track (people, companies, or both) and a time window (today, last 24 hours, a specific date range).
  2. The tool sweeps public sources — RSS feeds from major news outlets — and collects every headline mentioning a rostered entity.
  3. Deduplication. Syndicated and wire stories appear in many outlets under near-identical headlines. The tool clusters these so one AP story picked up by 12 outlets counts once, not twelve times.
  4. Evaluate a candidate brief. Paste or pipe in the text of a brief, and the tool scores it against the available-story universe.

The output is a coverage report:

MetricWhat it tells you
Coverage score (0–100)What fraction of available consensus stories appeared in the brief
Entity coverage breakdownFor each rostered entity: stories available vs. stories the brief mentioned
Syndication dedup ratioHow many raw headlines collapsed into how many unique stories (measures source diversity)
Missed-consensus reportStories carried by 2+ sources that the brief omitted — the most actionable output

Architecture

The system has four stages, each deliberately simple:

StageInputOutputHow
1. Roster loadJSON config fileEntity list with aliasesFlat file, no database
2. Source sweepEntity list + date windowRaw headline corpusRSS feeds via feedparser or stdlib XML
3. DedupRaw headlinesUnique story clustersTitle similarity (sequence matching)
4. EvaluationUnique stories + brief textCoverage reportEntity mention matching + story overlap scoring

Each stage is a pure function. You can swap out the source sweep (say, from RSS to a news API) without touching the evaluator. You can test the scoring logic without any network calls at all.

Scope discipline

I built the evaluator, not the generator. The evaluator is the interesting half; the generator is a commodity.

Any LLM can summarize ten articles into a brief. The hard part is knowing whether those ten articles were the right ten. That's a measurement problem, not a generation problem, and it's the one that scales: once you can score a brief's coverage, you can score any brief — written by a person, an AI, or a mix — and you can track quality over time.

These are structural results — the tool works as designed. Real-world accuracy metrics require running it against a production brief, which would involve non-public data. The architecture is built to make that measurement trivial once you plug in your own roster and sources.

What I chose not to build, and why

OmissionReason
A brief generatorCommodity problem. The evaluator is the differentiator — it tells you whether the generator did its job.
Real-time monitoringDifferent product with different infrastructure. This is a batch evaluation tool: run it once a day, after the brief is assembled, and get a score.
Multi-language supportEnglish sources only. Internationalization adds complexity without validating the core measurement idea.

What I'd build next

  • Historical trending. Store daily scores and chart coverage quality over weeks or months. Does coverage degrade when a team member is out? Does it improve after adding a source?
  • Source-quality scoring. Not all sources are equal. Weight coverage by outlet tier (wire services, major national, trade, blog) so a miss from Reuters counts more than a miss from a niche aggregator.
  • Roster tuning recommendations. If an entity never generates stories, suggest removing it. If a non-rostered entity keeps appearing in missed stories, suggest adding it.
  • News API integration. RSS is free and sufficient for a proof of concept. A production version would use a news API for better date filtering, richer metadata, and higher reliability.
