Product teardown + spec

Who's bringing what? Sign-up lists for WhatsApp Communities

WhatsApp can tell an organizer who's coming. It can't tell them who's doing what. This is a teardown of how Communities handles events today and a spec for closing that gap.

Type Independent spec workWritten September 2026Status Not validated with users yet

Short version. Events in WhatsApp now cover dates, RSVPs, plus-ones and reminders. The missing piece is the ask: who's on setup, who's bringing plates, who's driving. Organizers seem to handle that in chat or with outside sign-up apps. I'd add sign-up lists to events, measured on community stickiness with guardrails on notification fatigue. Everything below that isn't sourced is labeled as a hypothesis.

Why I picked this

I know community organizing from the board of Muslims for Progressive Values. The same coordination problem shows up for schools, neighborhoods, sports teams and faith groups. That's exactly who WhatsApp built Communities for. Meta's mission is to build the future of human connection and the technology that makes it possible, and this is connection in its most practical form: showing up for each other.

None of these announcements mention sign-up lists, task claiming or volunteer slots. Member tags are the closest thing: they describe who someone is in a group, not what they've signed up to do for a specific event.

The gap

An event answers "who's coming?" It doesn't answer "who's doing what?" For most community events, the second question is where the work is.

Evidence the job exists

  • There's a whole category of standalone sign-up and potluck apps built for this one job. Buyer's guides list at least seven for potlucks alone.
  • Nonprofit tooling guides describe volunteer coordination over WhatsApp as its own problem to solve with outside tools.

Workarounds I'd expect to find Hypothesis

  • A numbered list pasted into chat that each person copies, edits and reposts. It gets out of order fast.
  • Polls used as sign-ups. That works until a slot needs a cap ("two people on setup").
  • A link to an outside form, which means leaving the chat and often a login.

These need to be checked with organizers before anything gets built. That's the first step in the plan below.

Riskiest assumptions

  1. Organizers want this inside WhatsApp. They might prefer a spreadsheet they control. Test: interviews with 10 to 12 community organizers across schools, faith groups and sports teams.
  2. Clear asks bring members back. The DAU thesis depends on it. Test: compare activity for members who claimed a slot against similar members who didn't, in the pilot.
  3. It won't add noise. A list update could ping everyone. Test: mute and leave rates in the holdback test.

Options I considered

Option A

Upgrade polls

Add names and caps to poll options. Cheapest, but polls are for opinions. Stretching them muddles both jobs.

Option B · Chosen

Sign-up lists on events

Slots live on the event people already RSVP to. One place for who's coming and who's doing what.

Option C

Integrate outside apps

Let sign-up apps attach to events. Fast, but it sends people out of the chat and adds third-party data questions.

Why B: the event is already the object members open and RSVP to, and reminders already exist on it. Attaching slots there adds the missing piece without teaching anyone a new surface. The tradeoff is scope: B is more work than A, and it has to respect end-to-end encryption the way polls and events already do.

The spec

Goal

Help organizers get every role covered without leaving the chat, and give members a clear, low-effort way to contribute.

Non-goals

  • Payments, ticketing or anything that turns an event into a transaction.
  • Recurring volunteer scheduling. One event at a time for v1.
  • Lists outside of events.

Requirements

PriorityRequirement
P0Event creator can add a sign-up list with named slots and an optional cap per slot ("Setup, 2 people")
P0Members claim or unclaim a slot in one tap from the event card
P0Anyone can see which slots are open or full at a glance
P0Claims don't notify the whole group. Only the organizer gets a quiet summary
P0End-to-end encrypted, like polls and events. Names shown only to people already in the group
P1Organizer nudge for open slots, sent to members who RSVP'd yes but haven't claimed anything
P1Claimed slot appears in the member's event reminder ("You're on setup")
P2Templates for common lists: potluck, volunteer shifts, carpool

Edge cases

  • A member leaves the group after claiming a slot: the slot reopens and the organizer is told.
  • Two people tap the last spot at once: first confirmed claim wins; the other sees "just filled."
  • Older app versions: show the list as read-only text with a prompt to update.

How I'd measure it

These are planned metrics and targets, not results. Nothing here has shipped.

Test at the community level with a holdback group for eight weeks, since novelty can make week one look better than it is.

TypeMetricWhy
SuccessDAU/MAU and L28 active days for members of test communities vs. holdbackStickiness, not raw DAU, which a notification can inflate
DriverShare of communities that attach a list within 28 daysDo organizers adopt it?
DriverShare of RSVP'd members who claim a slot; slots filled before the eventDoes it get roles covered?
DriverMessages in the event's group in the week after itDid it create real conversation, not just a tap?
GuardrailMute and leave rates, notification opt-outs, spam reportsGrowth that annoys people isn't growth
GuardrailD28 retention, not D1Novelty fades
HealthRepeat use by organizers across eventsOrganizers bring everyone else back

Decision rule, set before launch: ship if test communities show a significant stickiness gain that holds through week eight, with no guardrail regression. Writing the rule first keeps us from rationalizing whatever the data shows.

Rollout

  1. Interviews with 10 to 12 organizers to check the workarounds and the "inside WhatsApp" assumption.
  2. Prototype test with a handful of real communities.
  3. Holdback test in a few markets where Communities use is high.
  4. Broad launch only if the decision rule is met.

What I don't know

  • How many community events actually need roles covered, versus just a headcount.
  • Whether WhatsApp is already testing something like this internally.
  • How organizers in different markets handle this today. My own view is mostly US nonprofit and community groups.
